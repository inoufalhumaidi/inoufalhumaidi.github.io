Selected work

Five self-directed analytics projects spanning public and synthetic operations data. Each includes a working artifact, transparent methodology, and a decision-focused case story.

A 40-SKU forecasting model, a clean feature pipeline, and a live accuracy-vs-stockout dashboard.

Output a weekly reorder signal per SKU, with the forecast error that produced it.

40 SKUs were reordered on gut feel and last month's sales, with no visibility into which items were about to run out or sit overstocked.

A Python forecasting pipeline covering seasonality, promotions and lead time, a SQL layer feeding governed KPIs, and an interactive dashboard ranking SKUs by forecast risk.

92.4% forecast accuracy at the SKU level cut stockouts by 18% and flagged 6 SKUs whose demand pattern had shifted enough to break the naive model — caught before they ran out.

A churn-risk model trained on usage and support signals, scored weekly for the retention team.

Output a ranked watchlist of accounts, with the top drivers behind each score.

Retention outreach happened only after a customer had already cancelled, with no earlier signal to act on.

A gradient-boosted classifier on engagement, billing and support-ticket features, validated against 18 months of history, feeding a weekly scored watchlist.

31% of eventual churners were flagged at least 60 days before cancelling. Acting on the top-risk tier projected an 8% retention lift against a holdout group.

A multi-touch attribution model reconciling ad spend, sessions and conversions into one source of truth.

Output a channel-level view of true incremental return, not last-click credit.

Each ad platform reported its own conversions, so channel budgets were argued from four contradictory numbers.

A SQL layer joining spend, sessions and orders, a data-driven attribution model, and DAX measures feeding one governed Power BI report.

Reconciling the numbers gave 4.2x clearer visibility into spend-to-revenue, and reallocating budget away from an over-credited channel toward two under-credited ones.

A ticket taxonomy, SLA rules and a triage tracker built from six months of support data.

Output a live view of every ticket's SLA clock and who owns the next action.

Tickets were tagged inconsistently, so nobody could say reliably which SLAs were actually being met.

A cleaned ticket taxonomy, explicit SLA rules by severity, and an Excel/SQL tracker showing the clock, owner and next action per ticket.

SLA traceability reached 97%, and standardizing the triage queue cut average time-to-first-response by 41%.

A series of checkout A/B tests analyzed for significance, guardrails and long-run stability.

Output a validated redesign recommendation, not just a p-value.

Past checkout tests were called "winners" from raw conversion counts alone, with no check for novelty effects or guardrail regressions.

A Python analysis pipeline with proper significance testing, pre-registered guardrail metrics (refunds, support tickets), and Plotly visualizations of the effect over time.

The winning variant held a 4.2% conversion lift after two weeks with no guardrail regression — validated before shipping to 100% of traffic.
